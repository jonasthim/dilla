# SP-09 Opus and SDP: DTX silence sent 50 packets/s until faa3dec; 4.7 packets/s after

Date: 2026-10-04. Host: Linux dev box (Arch, PipeWire running), Playwright 1.63.0, Chromium 153.0.8010.12, Firefox
155.0, LiveKit v1.13.7 in process, livekit-client 2.22.3 with the real `DillaE2EEManager` (task 17, harness
`e2ee: 'dilla'`), connected directly to LiveKit with debug tokens (MD-13). Spec: `e2e/spikes/opus-sdp.spec.ts`.
Every number below comes from two complete runs of that spec on 2026-10-04 (run 1 and run 2, each `8 passed`,
`8 skipped`, 14.8 min), made outside any sandbox. Where the runs agree, one number is given; where they differ,
both are.

> **Superseded DTX conclusion (2026-10-05).** Commit `faa3dec` passes the encoder's 0-byte Opus frame through as an empty frame, without spending a counter. The earlier silence rows and decision 5 below describe the pre-change worker. They are retained as historical measurements. Packet absence now reveals silence in addition to packet size.

## After faa3dec — current DTX wire measurement

The committed `e2e/spikes/opus-sdp.spec.ts` disabled the microphone's underlying `MediaStreamTrack` (`enabled = false`) for the silent phase. It sampled RTP and worker counters after a settling interval. On **Chromium 153, n=1 run on 2026-10-05**, the silent phase emitted **4.7 RTP packets/s**, **96.9 payload bytes/s**, **4.7 encrypted nonempty frames/s**, **45.35 empty frames/s** and **4.7 authenticated receiver frames/s** over **20 s**. The active phase emitted **50 packets/s** and **50 encrypted frames/s** over **10 s**. The silence line printed by that run was:

The Firefox 155 leg in this sandbox did not yield a measurement: `window.harness.session()` became null before the wire-counter read. ## Question

Do dilla's Opus publish options produce on the wire what DEV-08 and ruling F2 assume (spec L494: Opus 48 kHz,
20 ms, DTX, in-band FEC, 64 kbps; screen audio 96 kbps stereo), and is gap G40's stereo trap live?

## How the answer fmtp is decided (read from the pinned sources, then measured)

- livekit-client 2.22.3 `LocalParticipant.publishTrack` sends `TF_STEREO` whenever the **source** track
  (`getSourceTrackSettings()`, before any processor) reports `channelCount > 1` (`LocalParticipant.ts:1086-1088`).
  `forceStereo` does not affect that flag; it only feeds `isStereo`, which defaults `dtx` and `red` to off
  (`LocalParticipant.ts:904-926`), and `disableDtx = !(opts.dtx ?? true)` adds `TF_NO_DTX`.
- LiveKit v1.13.7 rewrites the Opus fmtp of its answer only when it finds the track's info for that m-section
  (`participant_sdp.go`, the `ti == nil → continue` branch before the rewrite): `;usedtx=1` unless `TF_NO_DTX`,
  `;stereo=1;maxaveragebitrate=510000` with `TF_STEREO`, `;stereo=1` removed otherwise. When it does not find the
  info, the answer keeps pion's own fmtp (`minptime=10;stereo=1;useinbandfec=1`, no `usedtx`).
- The spec records the server's `trackInfo.audioFeatures` for every trap row and marks an answer **configured**
  when its `stereo=1` and `usedtx=1` match `TF_STEREO` and `TF_NO_DTX`.

## DTX silence through the real manager on the wire

A two-party call (A publishes, B subscribes and decrypts) with task 19's capture
`{ echoCancellation: true, noiseSuppression: true, autoGainControl: true }`, `MIC`, and a processor whose output is
a 1 kHz oscillator behind a GainNode (0 = digital silence, 1 = tone). Deltas over each phase after 2 s to settle.
The context was `running` with its clock advancing in every phase. No drop at A in any phase.

| engine | phase | seconds | RTP packets/s | payload B/s | B per packet | encrypted frames/s | decrypted at B /s |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Chromium | silence | 20 | 50.05 / 49.95 | 960.55 / 958.65 | 19.19 | 50.05 | 50.05 |
| Chromium | tone | 10 | 50.30 / 50.10 | 9054 / 9018 | 180.00 | 50.20 / 49.90 | 50.20 / 49.90 |
| Firefox | silence | 20 | 50.00 | 1132.40 | 22.65 | 50.00 | 50.00 |
| Firefox | tone | 10 | 49.90 / 50.20 | 8982 / 9036 | 180.00 | 49.90 / 50.20 | 50.20 |

Answer fmtp in this leg: Chromium `minptime=10;usedtx=1;useinbandfec=1`, Firefox
`maxplaybackrate=48000;stereo=0;useinbandfec=1;usedtx=1;maxaveragebitrate=64000` — DTX was negotiated. The dilla
worker encrypts every frame it is given, including the encoder's 0-byte DTX frames (`packages/media/src/worker/
pipeline.ts`, "nothing is ever passed through unencrypted"), so each becomes a 19–23 B SFrame payload and is sent.
The padded loopback row reproduces this without the worker (50.3–50.45 packets/s). **With dilla's E2EE, DTX saves
bytes (≈ 1 kB/s in silence against ≈ 9 kB/s of speech-level audio) but no packets: a silent participant sends
50 packets/s.** Silence remains visible to the SFU and to a passive observer from packet size (19–23 B against
180 B), not from packet timing.

Reading the table:

1. **The trap as G40 e described it (processed 2-channel track → stereo) is not live with dilla's explicit
   options:** wherever the explicit `MIC` (or `{}` = `ROOM_DEFAULTS`) is used, the destination's channel count
   changes nothing in the configured answer — compare every `default (2)` row with its `channelCount = 1` twin.
2. **What does decide stereo is the source.** Chromium's capture with processing off reports two channels, so
   livekit-client sends `TF_STEREO` and LiveKit answers `stereo=1;maxaveragebitrate=510000` whatever
   `forceStereo`, the destination or a `channelCount: 1` capture constraint says. With processing on
   (`MIC_CAPTURE`) the Chromium source is mono and the answer is `usedtx=1` without `stereo=1`. Firefox's fake
   source is mono either way.
3. **Without explicit options** ("unset") Chromium also loses DTX (`TF_NO_DTX`), in both destination variants;
   why the mono-destination row is classified stereo by `isStereoInput` was not isolated. Firefox, which exposes no
   processed `channelCount`, keeps DTX.
4. **Unconfigured answers.** In 16 of 160 trap samples (fresh rooms: Chromium 4 of 60, Firefox 6 of 60; reused
   room: Chromium 2 of 20, Firefox 4 of 20) and in one of the two Chromium screen-share publications, LiveKit answered a new audio
   m-section with pion's fmtp (`stereo=1`, no `usedtx`, no `maxaveragebitrate=510000`) although the server's track
   info said otherwise. It happened in both engines, both orders (including task 19's), and both room shapes, so it
   is a race at publish time, not a property of any row; it is what made the first run's matrix look
   run-dependent. Candidate (not verified): livekit-client runs `engine.addTrack(req)` and `negotiate()`
   concurrently when the server announced publish video codecs (`LocalParticipant.ts`, `Promise.all([addTrackPromise,
   negotiate()])`), so the offer can be answered before the `AddTrackRequest` is pending. The sender caps
   (`encodings[0].maxBitrate`) were unaffected; until the next renegotiation such a publication encodes with
   `stereo=1` and without DTX (the mic) or without the 510000 target (screen audio).

## Decision task 19 consumes

1. `MIC` = `{ source: Track.Source.Microphone, audioPreset: { maxBitrate: 64_000, priority: 'high' }, dtx: true,
   red: false, forceStereo: false }` — **held**, paired with the processing-on `MIC_CAPTURE`
   (`{ echoCancellation: true, noiseSuppression: true, autoGainControl: true }`) task 19 already writes. Not every
   microphone check passed: with processing off, Chromium's 2-channel source makes the answer
   `stereo=1;maxaveragebitrate=510000` (`forceStereo: false` cannot prevent that), while `usedtx=1`, FEC, no RED,
   no `a=ptime` and the 64 000 cap held in both engines. With `MIC_CAPTURE` every check passed in both engines.
2. `SCREEN_AUDIO` = `{ source: Track.Source.ScreenShareAudio, audioPreset: { maxBitrate: 96_000 }, forceStereo: true,
   dtx: false, red: false }` — **held** (Chromium display capture; Firefox synthetic publication only).
3. The RNNoise processor's destination node **sets `channelCount = 1` anyway**: no `default (2)` row with explicit
   `MIC` and task 19's capture shows `stereo=1` or lacks `usedtx=1` in a configured answer, and the explicit
   `forceStereo: false` / `dtx: true` fully neutralise the 2-channel processed track. The only `default (2)` +
   explicit `MIC` rows with `stereo=1` are the processing-off capture rows, and their `channelCount = 1` twins show
   the same `stereo=1`: the source decides it, the destination cannot. The mono destination keeps the processed
   track itself mono (Chromium reported 2 channels for the default destination, 1 for the mono one).
4. Opus packet interval: **20 ms** (50.0–50.3 packets/s of tone in Chromium, 49.9–52.4 in Firefox; 49.9–50.02
   frames/s in the transform); no `a=ptime` munging is needed (**confirmed**: no `a=ptime` or `a=maxptime` in any
   answer or offer).
5. DTX frames: the sender transform sees **2706 (Chromium) / 2704 (Firefox) 0-byte and 143 1–2-byte frames per 60 s of
   digital silence**, plus 152 larger ones; the dilla worker encrypts every frame it is given, including these
   (Opus prefix 0 bytes). Measured consequence, contrary to the pre-written expectation that DTX leaves gaps: an
   encrypted 0-byte frame is a 19–23 B payload and is sent, so **a dilla call in silence sends 50 packets/s per
   microphone**, not the ≈ 5 packets/s of unencrypted DTX. A passive observer sees silence from packet size, not
   from gaps.

## Differences from the first run of this spike (commit 728d008) and its supervisor re-run

- The first run reported Firefox's `AudioContext` as `suspended` and its tone phase as silent. In both runs here
  the context was `running` and its clock advanced in every phase (the supervisor's re-run also saw `running`).
  The first run was made inside the Codex sandbox; most likely the audio server was unreachable there (not
  verified). Its Firefox tone and real-wire tone rows are superseded by the numbers above.
- The first run's trap matrix used one reused room and showed rows whose `stereo`/`usedtx` flags changed between
  runs (Chromium `MIC_CAPTURE` + default destination `stereo=1` in that run, `stereo=0` in the re-run). Those were
  unconfigured answers (point 4 above), not destination effects; the matrix now runs every row in fresh rooms,
  repeats it, and checks each answer against the server's track info.
- Real-manager Chromium silence: 959.6 B/s (first run), about 1100 B/s (supervisor re-run), 958.65–960.55 B/s
  here; the packet rate was about 50/s every time. The cause of the supervisor's higher figure was not isolated.

## Limits

- Not run here: Firefox real `getDisplayMedia` (headless Firefox has none; a synthetic publication stands in),
  Safari, macOS, Windows, Electron, a second LAN host.
- All capture is fake capture: Chromium's file-backed fake microphone and fake display audio, Firefox's fake
  streams. Whether a real stereo microphone with processing on reports one channel in every Chromium build was
  not measured.
- The padded loopback row models the SFrame overhead with 19 zero bytes; it isolates the packet-rate effect, not
  the exact payload size.
