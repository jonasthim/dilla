# dilla v0.1.0-web3.10

Released 2026-10-21.

A client-only release that reworks the member list and adds a profile card, from Jonas's notes on the deployed web3.9: why the name was not vertically aligned, why the member list needed "owner" and "you" pills, and what the call dock should show. Nothing about messages, keys or calls changes on the wire.

## What's new

- **A member row is the avatar and the name, centred.** The tags ("owner", "you", "bot") and the "…" actions button are gone from the row. While a person has no status line, the name is centred in the row; with one, the row has two lines.
- **Role-coloured names.** A member's name is drawn in the colour of their highest coloured role. When that colour would not reach a 4.5:1 contrast in your theme, the name keeps the normal text colour. The colour dot after the name is gone.
- **A crown for the owner.** A small crown after the owner's name, named "owner" for screen readers.
- **A profile card.** Click a member row, press Enter on it, right-click it, or press Shift+F10 (or the ContextMenu key), and it opens that person's card: a large avatar, name, @username, status, "owner" and "you" where they apply, the roles as coloured chips, and the actions. On a computer it is a popover beside the row; on a phone it is a sheet from the bottom. Escape or **close** shuts it and returns focus to the row; Tab stays inside it.
- **Actions in the card.** **message** and **compare safety numbers** (not on your own card), **edit roles** for people who manage roles (it was **roles** in the old menu), and **kick** and **ban**. Kick and ban follow rank: they are offered only when your roles allow them and the person's highest role is below yours, and never on the owner or on yourself. The server still enforces both.
- **Mentions and authors open the card.** A user mention in a message is a button that opens the same card under it, in channels and in threads. So does a message's author name or avatar. Tab reaches them inside the message, Enter opens the card and Escape closes it and returns focus. Role mentions and @everyone are not clickable.
- **The call dock names the channel.** Line 1 is the channel you are in (hover it for the server's name), replacing "voice connected". Line 2 is your link: ping and ↑/↓ kb/s, like "24 ms · ↑48 ↓96 kb/s", read on your own device every 2 seconds and never sent to the server. Before the first reading it says "— ms"; while connecting, reconnecting or paused it shows those words instead.
- **Quality colours.** The figures use the normal colour under 150 ms, the warning colour from 150 to 300 ms, and the danger colour above 300 ms or with more than 5 % loss. A screen reader hears "good", "poor" or "bad" with the numbers, and the numbers are not announced as they change.
- **Dock controls on the right.** Camera, share and leave sit together at the right end of the dock, in that order.
- **The channel you are in looks like Discord's.** Its row no longer has a phone icon or an accent-coloured speaker; the name turns bold, and a screen reader hears "connected".
- **Room for the mute and deafen icons.** A person's icons under a voice channel have a gap between them and end in line with the channel row's right edge, no longer touching the list's edge.
- **AFK is always last.** The AFK channel is the very last row of the channel list, below every category and in none, whatever its stored position. Folding a category never hides it, and the keyboard jump between channels follows the same order. Nothing is changed on the server.
- **The WEB tag is always shown.** A message from a browser shows its WEB tag all the time, so pointing at a message no longer shifts its text. A grouped message (no head) still shows no tag.
- **The sidebar's resize line runs the full height.** The border between the channel list and the conversation reaches from the top of the app to the bottom, beside the call dock and the user panel too, and can be dragged from anywhere along it.
- **Status words in lower case.** The status menu and the member list say online, idle, do not disturb, invisible and offline.

## Operator notes

- **Client-only.** Nothing to configure and no server change. The new browser client comes inside the `dillad` binary, so it is still a `dillad` upgrade: `install.sh --upgrade --version v0.1.0-web3.10`. Open tabs pick up the new build and reload.
- **Check for live calls before you upgrade.** Restarting `dillad` restarts the media server (SFU), which disconnects every live call. Run `dillad media --rooms --config=/etc/dilla/dilla.toml` first and upgrade when it reports `no live rooms`. An idle desktop parked in the AFK channel counts as a live call, so an empty-looking server can still be listed there.
- The dock's ping and rates are read in the browser from its own connection and are not sent anywhere.
- **Rollback to web3.9.2** is safe. A web3.9.2 tab shows the older member list and dock.

## Known limits

- Direct messages show plain mentions. A profile card in a direct message is a follow-up.
- A former member's mention is clickable only while one of their messages is held in this browser. Their card has the name only, with no roles and no actions.
- The card on a phone's thread sheet is covered by a jsdom test and by `threads.spec`, not by a phone end-to-end test.
- There is still no profile you can edit: the card shows what the server knows (name, username, status, roles).
- The web3.9, web3.9.1 and web3.9.2 known limits still hold.
