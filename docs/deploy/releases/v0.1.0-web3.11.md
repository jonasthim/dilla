# dilla v0.1.0-web3.11

Released 2026-10-21.

AFK now means disconnected. Until now, an idle desktop was moved into a listen-only call in the AFK channel, which kept a media room open for people who were not there. From this release the AFK channel never hosts a call: the people in it are listed as **away**, with no voice connection at all. The release also changes the installer so that `install.sh --upgrade` refuses to restart the media server while a call is live.

> **The `dillad.prev` swap is not the rollback for this release.** The database moves to schema 21 at the first start, and web3.10.1 refuses to start on it. Roll back with `migrate down` first, as described in [Operator notes](#operator-notes).

## What's new

### Exercised end to end by tests

These are proved by the browser suite (`e2e/web/afk.spec.ts`, from an empty instance, in Chromium and Firefox; the AFK suite does not run in WebKit) or, for the installer, by `deploy/install_test.sh` against a stand-in `dillad` and `systemctl`.

- **No AFK call.** Nobody joins a call in the AFK channel, and it opens no media (LiveKit) room. An idle desktop no longer keeps a room open, so the load on the media server drops and an empty server really is empty.
- **The away list.** The AFK row lists everyone who is away in that server, each with the word "away" after the name, and counts them ("2 in AFK"). It is kept up to date live. The AFK channel stays the very last row of the channel list, below every category.
- **The AFK page.** Opening the AFK channel shows the same list, or "Nobody is away.", and a **go AFK** button. Once you are away, the button is replaced by "You’re away. Join a voice channel, or send a message or type in this server, to come back."
- **Go AFK yourself.** Click the AFK row, or press **go AFK** on its page. You leave any call you are in and are listed as away.
- **"Moved to AFK" for the sweep.** After the inactive timeout, a computer idle in a call is disconnected, listed as away, and its screen says "Moved to AFK for inactivity" with the AFK channel's name. Nothing joins, and no microphone is asked for.
- **"You went AFK on another device."** When you choose AFK on one device, your other devices' calls end with this calm notice, not a "removed" error (shown in the suite with an emulated phone beside a desktop).
- **What brings you back.** Away ends when you join a voice channel, or when you send a message in that server; closing your last connection takes you off the list once 30 seconds pass with no connection (the suite runs this on the test host's clock), and coming back joins nothing.
- **Wording.** The settings no longer say people in AFK are "muted": the inactive-timeout hint now says members "are disconnected and listed as away", and the AFK channel's edit dialog says "Nobody is connected here; people who are away are listed."
- **Installer: refuses to restart live calls.** `install.sh --upgrade` now runs `dillad media --rooms` as the service user, with the installed config, at the moment it is about to stop anything. If a room has participants it prints the rooms and their participant counts and exits with an error, having changed nothing. If the check itself fails, it refuses too. `--yes` upgrades anyway, with a warning. When `dilla-media` is not running at all (for example after `systemctl stop dilla`, as in the rollback below), there can be no live call: the installer says so and goes on without `--yes`. An instance still running the old all-in-one SFU (`livekit.mode = 'in_process'`) cannot be asked, so its first upgrade also needs `--yes`: that upgrade ends every live call.

### Not yet seen working on a real instance

These are covered by unit and server tests, and some by emulated browsers in the suite, but nobody has yet watched them on real devices against a deployed instance.

- **A long laptop sleep.** Usually the media server drops a sleeping computer within seconds, long before the inactive timeout, so the sweep never disconnects it and nobody is listed away; on waking it rejoins its call as after any network loss, with the microphone. Only a computer that the sweep disconnected before it slept wakes, reads the AFK state first, and joins nothing.
- **A phone plus a desktop.** A real phone is never disconnected for being idle, while the desktop beside it in the same call is, and is not listed away for it; going AFK on the phone ends the desktop's call with "You went AFK on another device."
- **Typing brings you back,** as a message does; a message or typing in a direct message does not.
- **The 30 s offline grace,** on a real clock and real networks (the suite runs it on the test host's clock). A page reload or a short network drop keeps you away; having no connection at all for 30 seconds (every tab and device closed or offline) takes you off the list.
- **Your other devices leave their calls** when you go AFK on one, across real separate machines rather than browser contexts on one host.

## Operator notes

- **Schema 21.** Migration 00021 adds one table, `community_afk_away`: one row per member who is away in a server. The server also gains one route, `PUT /v1/communities/{id}/afk/away`. Nothing else is stored.
- **Rollback is not the usual `dillad.prev` swap.** The database is at schema 21 after the first start, and web3.10.1 refuses to start on a database newer than its own migrations. Putting the old binary back as it is leaves it refusing. Take the database down first, **with the new binary** (only it knows the way down), and then reinstall web3.10.1 with this release's `install.sh`:

  ```sh
  systemctl stop dilla
  runuser -u dillad -- /usr/local/bin/dillad migrate down --to=20 --i-know-what-i-am-doing --config=/etc/dilla/dilla.toml
  install.sh --upgrade --version v0.1.0-web3.10.1
  ```

  `systemctl stop dilla` also stops `dilla-media` (its unit is `PartOf=dilla.service`), so no call is live. The installer sees that `dilla-media` is not running, says "dilla-media is not running, so there are no live calls", and goes on without `--yes`. It reads schema 20 with the installed binary, puts web3.10.1's `dillad` and `dilla_core_wasi.wasm` in place (keeping this release's pair as `.prev`), starts `dilla-media` and restarts `dilla`; no separate `systemctl start` is needed. To roll back by hand instead, after the same two first steps: `cp -p /usr/local/bin/dillad.prev /usr/local/bin/dillad; cp -p /usr/local/bin/dilla_core_wasi.wasm.prev /usr/local/bin/dilla_core_wasi.wasm; systemctl start dilla` (the `.prev` pair is web3.10.1's only if this release was installed with `install.sh --upgrade` and nothing was upgraded since).

  The down step drops `community_afk_away`; web3.9 to web3.10.1 would ignore the table anyway. The only thing lost is the away list, which refills as people go AFK. If the new binary fails to start during `install.sh --upgrade`, the installer already runs this step itself before it puts the old binary back.
- **At the first start, live AFK calls end.** Any call still running in an AFK channel (web3.9 to web3.10.1 kept them) is ended, and the people in it are listed as away. A person who has no connection is dropped from the list again about a minute after the start.
- **The AFK channel never opens a LiveKit room.** A start there and a media token for it are refused (`403`). A server where everyone is idle now has no rooms.
- **The container image is no longer built or published; use install.sh.** Existing GHCR images stay but get no updates. `Dockerfile` and `deploy/compose.yaml` are gone from the repository.
- **Check `dillad media --rooms` immediately before the restart (the answer goes stale), and confirm it in the API journal, with your grep pattern first checked against a window known to contain a call.** Restarting `dillad` restarts the media server (SFU), which disconnects every live call. The installer now runs the check itself, right before it stops the service, and refuses while a room has participants (`--yes` overrides). By hand: `dillad media --rooms --config=/etc/dilla/dilla.toml` prints `no live rooms`, or each room with its participant count; one taken ten minutes earlier proves nothing. To confirm it, every attached device posts to `calls/{id}/stats` every 10 s, so no such posts in the last 2 minutes means no attached device. Before you trust that zero, run the same grep over a minute in which you were in a call yourself and see that it finds your posts: a pattern that silently matches nothing looks the same as an empty server.
- **Upgrading from web3.10.1, the AFK channel's calls count as live.** On web3.10.1 an idle desktop sits in the AFK channel's listen-only call for as long as it stays open, and `dillad media --rooms` lists that room with its participants (room ids only, so it looks like any other call). If the only rooms left are those idle people in AFK, upgrading with `--yes` is expected: their calls end at the first start anyway, and they are listed as away.
- **The internal health probe uses the `plain_listen` address.** `dillad` binds only `server.plain_listen`, not the loopback address, so `curl http://127.0.0.1:8080/healthz` returns `000` unless `plain_listen` is exactly that. Use the address in `server.plain_listen`, for example `curl -fsS http://10.0.0.5:8080/healthz`.
- **The new browser client comes inside the `dillad` binary.** Open tabs pick up the new build and reload.

## Known limits

- **Old tabs.** A tab from web3.9 to web3.10.1 that is still open ignores op 55 and the away list. Its AFK snapshot also fails to decode (an AFK decode error) for a device cut by a chosen AFK (`pending` = 2) until the tab reloads. The new build reloads open tabs, so this is brief. The person sees "You were removed from voice." instead of the calm line, and the tab keeps retrying the AFK read; if it then goes offline before its next join, every community voice join fails until it reloads. In short: a tab from before web3.11 may be unable to join community voice until it reloads. Reload any tab that was open across the upgrade; the new build normally reloads open tabs on its own. Until it reloads, pressing AFK in such a tab gets a `403` join failure.
- **A failed cut is retried, for a while.** When you go AFK and the media server fails to list its rooms or to remove one of your devices, you are listed as away while that device stays in its call. The server retries the cut on each maintenance pass (every `gateway.heartbeat_interval`, 30 s by default) while you are still away, at most five times, and logs each retry; when one goes through, the overlap ends within about two and a half minutes. If every retry fails, the device stays in its call until it leaves or rejoins on its own, which takes you off the away list.
- **The away list holds the first 1024** people of a server. A server with more away at once lists only those.
- **Why a device left its call is forgotten on a restart.** The reason a device was cut ("Moved to AFK", or "You went AFK on another device") is held in memory. A computer swept just before an upgrade that wakes after it is not told why it left, and its call recovery may join the call again. The away list itself is stored and survives the restart.
- **A person removed from a server** (a kick or leave at the same instant as an admission or a disconnect) may still be shown as away to the others until they next read the list.
- **One `dillad` process per database.** The away index lives in memory. A backup restored under a running instance is cleared only after the next restart.
- **Messages in direct messages do not bring you back.** A message or typing in a server's text channel does; one in a direct message does not.
- **Away notices are not queued across a restart.** Every client reads the list afresh when it reconnects, so nothing is lost, but a notice not yet sent at shutdown is dropped.
- **Timing at the first start.** Marking after a restart does not check who is still connected; the sweep a minute after the start corrects it.
- **The AFK page is a list, not a stage.** It shows the away people and the **go AFK** button, with no tiles.
- **Deciding whether another of your devices is in a call** lists the media server's rooms once per cut device. This is fine at self-hosted scale.
- The web3.10.1 known limits still hold.

Today the check is `grep -aoE 'assets/index-[A-Za-z0-9_-]{8}\.(js|css)'` on the binary and on the served shell. Not implemented here.
- The installer counting recent `calls/{id}/stats` posts itself, with a pattern it tests, so operators do not have to grep.
