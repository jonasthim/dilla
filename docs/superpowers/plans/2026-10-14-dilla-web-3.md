**Goal:** The web client should support everything below:
- See who is in voice and join a voice channel.
- Call someone from a DM. Their open tabs ring with an in-app answer flow and a desktop notification.
- Talk with mute, deafen and push-to-talk, and hear join and leave sounds.
- Share a camera or a screen.
- Compare a call code, and leave.
- Play audio and video attachments inline.

The call's media keys come only from the call group the browser core holds. The browser refuses to join, or stops deriving keys, wherever the core's send block is set. Before the voice screens, a probe measures the founder's real network path, run together with him.

**Architecture:**
- **Core worker.** It owns the call group: it finds it through a new read route, registers it or joins it by external commit, and leaves it with a self-`Remove`. It derives each epoch's `base_key` through one Rust export that refuses whenever `send_block_code` is set.
- **Page.** It owns the LiveKit `Room` through `@dilla/media`'s existing `joinCall`. It receives each key once, as a transferred buffer, and hands it to the media worker.
- **dillad.** It gains two read routes (call discovery and a voice snapshot), a server-side ring for DM calls (S→C gateway op 53 and a decline route), and the `Permissions-Policy` and `media-src blob:` widenings.
- **UI and web.** `packages/ui` gets presentational voice, ring and media-player components, and `@dilla/web` wires them into the Mesh shell. Sounds are synthesised with WebAudio, so no sound files ship.
- **Probe.** A standalone network probe (a testkit subcommand plus a small page in `packages/media`) measures the founder's homelab path, and its result goes into the deploy guide.
- **Tests.** A voice e2e suite runs two real browsers through a real SFU, plus a native peer that can poison a call group.

## Founder decisions this plan implements (binding)

1. Shortcuts are Ctrl/⌘+Shift+M (mute) and Ctrl/⌘+Shift+D (deafen). There are no single-key shortcuts.
2. Voice activity is the default. Push-to-talk is opt-in, has no default key, and works only while the dilla tab has focus.
3. `base_key` passes through the page as a transferred buffer, zeroed after use and never in a slice. A direct worker-to-worker port stays a card.
4. A DM call is refused when either the DM's call group or its text group is send-blocked.
5. The wasm gate rises to 3,500,000 B. The media worker reuses the core wasm, so it is downloaded once.
6. **DM calls ring**: a desktop notification plus an in-app ring and answer flow (task 3 for the server, task 6 for the worker, tasks 7–9 for UI and sound).
7. **Inline audio and video attachments are in web-3** (task 1 contract, tasks 3, 7, 9 and 10).
8. **Join and leave sounds are in web-3, with a setting to turn them off** (tasks 8 and 9).
9. The call code (6×5 digits from `epoch_authenticator`) sits behind a closed disclosure on the call stage.
10. Camera and screen share are both in. Screen audio is Chromium tab audio only.
11. **A real-network voice test runs on Jonas's homelab early, together with Jonas** (task 2, lane E). It asks whether LiveKit UDP needs its own port past Pangolin, and its result feeds the deploy guide.

**Never written.** This list adds to everything web-1 through web-2c forbid.
- **A `base_key`, or any byte of one, in any of these places:** a slice, a log, `console`, a stats object, a `data-*` attribute, IndexedDB, OPFS, `localStorage`, a URL or a `BroadcastChannel`. It may travel only in two `postMessage`s: the `callKeys` push from the core worker to its own page, and `installEpoch` from the page to its media worker. Both transfer the `ArrayBuffer`. **No command result (`ret`) ever carries key bytes**: the join epoch's key is a `callKeys` push too, posted immediately before `callJoin`'s `ret` (review fix: a `ret` is structured-cloned without a transfer list, which would leave an un-zeroed copy in the worker). In the worker the core-port adapter zero-fills the bytes decoded from the core and copies the key into a fresh `ArrayBuffer`, which the transfer detaches (`byteLength === 0` after posting). The client bridge zero-fills after its listeners (decision 3), and `DillaE2EEManager.installEpoch` already zero-fills the page copy it is given (`packages/media/src/manager.ts:269`).
- A media key derived anywhere but `ClientCore::call_media_epoch`, or derived while `send_block_code(call group)` is `Some`, while any non-own leaf of the call group lacks an `Ok` `leaf_verdict` against a cached signed list (pending counts as a refusal, ruling RV-1), or, for a call group whose binding has no community (a DM or group DM), while `send_block_code` of that DM's text group is `Some` (decision 4, enforced in the core, not only in the worker).
- A call join that posts an external commit after the joined tree showed a blocking leaf.
- A LiveKit token, ICE credential or call id in a slice. These live in command results only.
- livekit-client or RNNoise in `@dilla/client-core` or in the web entry chunk.
- A second core wasm download.
- A single-character shortcut (WCAG 2.1.4).
- A ring for a DM whose call group or text group is send-blocked (decision 4 extended to rings).
- A ring sound or notification while the person is already in a call.
- A sound for the person's own join, leave, mute or deafen.
- A sound while deafened.
- A `callKeys` post by the worker, or a key install by the page, while the call phase is `paused` (RV-3).

**Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA, keyboard first):**
- **Baseline.** Tokens come only from `packages/design-tokens`. Support three themes plus reduced motion. Each state gets a story. `vitest-axe` covers the components; the Storybook axe gate runs with `target-size` (24×24 CSS px) and the focus-ring guard. Use `aria-disabled` rather than `disabled`.
- **Speaking.** It is shown as an outline plus the word `speaking`. The 2 s pulse runs only without `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. The 12-bar meter always redraws every 120 ms.
- **Other participant states.** Muted, deafened, camera and screen are shown as a glyph plus a word.
- **Announcements.** One `role="status"` region in the dock announces join, leave, mute and deafen.
- **Ringing.** An incoming ring is announced once through `role="alert"`. It never moves focus. Its answer and decline buttons are the first controls in the main landmark until the ring ends.
- **Toolbars.** The dock (`aria-label` "voice controls") and the stage controls (`aria-label` "call controls") are toolbars with roving focus.
- **Tiles.** Participant tiles form a `role="list"`. The per-person volume is a native `<input type="range" min=0 max=200 step=10>`.
- **Shortcuts.** Control/⌘+Shift+M and Control/⌘+Shift+D, while in voice. Push-to-talk acts only while its key is held and the page has focus, and its handler calls `preventDefault` so it never types.
- **Sounds.** Each is under 300 ms (brief § Sound). The ring is a 2 × 120 ms ping every 2 s for at most 30 s. All of them obey Settings → Notifications → sounds.
- **Attachment players.** Use native `<audio controls>` and `<video controls playsinline>`, so the browser's keyboard-operable controls apply. The accessible name is the file's shown name. Playback starts only on an explicit play. Captions do not exist for user uploads; the user guide states this limitation (WCAG 1.2.x applies to authored media).

- **Mechanism, not duration (lesson c).**
  - Wait for a state, never sleep.
  - `WAIT` is at least 30 s locally and 90 s in CI.
  - **LiveKit shows an existing participant a joiner about 2.9 s late.** livekit-server v1.13.7's `subscriberUpdateInterval` is 3 s (media card 23). So the UI shows presence from dillad's `voice_state`, and tests wait for `data-voice-state="ok"`.
  - A ring's 30 s timeout is tested with dillad's fake clock in Go, and in the browser by waiting for the ring-ended state, never by asserting how long it took.
- **Close every context you open (lesson d, the leaked-contexts lesson).**
  - Contexts open only through `e2e/web/support/second.ts` or `e2e/voice/support/peer-browser.ts`, and close in `finally`.
  - A `Room` is disconnected and its media worker terminated before the context closes.
  - `e2e/voice/support/leaks.ts` asserts in `afterEach` that `browser.contexts().length` is back to its `beforeEach` value.
- **Firefox needs an audio server headlessly.** CI runs `sh scripts/ci-audio-server.sh` (a PulseAudio null sink) before Firefox. Without it the `AudioContext` stays `suspended`, and every analyser and every synthesised sound reads silent. Each Firefox audio assertion first asserts `AudioContext.state === 'running'`. ### Wire and HTTP (task 1 writes them; task 3 implements the server)

- **L-HTTP-100** `GET /v1/channels/{id}/call` → `200 [group_id(bstr16|null), call_id(bstr16|null), participants([[user_id(bstr16), device_id(bstr16), flags(uint)]])]`.
  - **Access:** callers with `view_channel`, or the participants of a DM or group DM. Anyone else gets `404 E_NOT_FOUND`. Kind 0 (text) or kind 2 (category) is `400 E_INVALID_REQUEST`.
  - **`group_id`:** the live call's group. With no live call, the channel's newest open `call` group (`Calls.callGroup`, `internal/api/calls.go:728`). `null` when there is none, which means "register one".

  - **`participants`:** the devices in the live call's room with their current `voice_state` flags, sorted by `user_id` then `device_id`, at most `livekit.max_voice_participants`.
  - **Bucket:** `read`.
- **L-HTTP-101** `GET /v1/communities/{id}/calls` → `200 [[channel_id, call_id, participants]]`: the live calls of the voice channels the caller may view, sorted by `channel_id`, unpaginated. A non-member gets `404`. Bucket: `read`.

- **L-HTTP-103** (client use of an existing route) `POST /v1/groups/{id}/proposal` `[epoch, proposal]` → `[seq]`. A `409 E_REMOVE_PENDING` means "being removed": withdraw and do not retry.
- **L-HTTP-104** `POST /v1/calls/{call_id}/decline` `[expires_ts(uint)]` → `204`.
  - **Why the body:** a call id is the channel id for every call of that channel (`internal/ds/state.go:458` `callIDOf`; `09` § Voice), so without it a late decline of an earlier ring would stop the next ring of the same DM and tell its caller "declined". The decline acts only on the ring whose `expires_ts` equals the body's; any other value is `204` with no effect.
  - **Who may call it:** only a participant of the call's DM or group DM. Anyone else, an unknown call, or an ended call gets `404 E_NOT_FOUND`. A call of a community voice channel gets `400 E_INVALID_REQUEST`. A malformed body is `400 E_INVALID_REQUEST`.
  - **Repeats:** idempotent, `204` whether or not the user was still ringing.
  - **Effect:** stops that user's ring (op 53 state 0 to every device of that user) and sends op 53 state 2 to every device of the caller.
  - **Bucket:** `write`.
- **L-HTTP-105** (behaviour, no new route; RV-2, Jonas) **Idle call-group close.** Every 60 s (instance clock) the instance closes, through the delivery service as `closeCallGroup` does (`calls.go:672`), each open `call` group (kind 1) that **no live voice session names** (`voice_sessions.group_id` with `ended` null) and whose `created` is at least **600 s** ago. At most 1,024 groups per pass, oldest first; the rest wait for the next pass. A closed group answers as today (`403 E_LEAF_NOT_CURRENT` at a start; L-HTTP-100 no longer names it, so `group_id` becomes `null` or a newer open group), and the next joiner registers a fresh call group. A start that read the group open and writes its row after the close is ended at once by the existing CALLS-2 re-read and answers `503 E_UNAVAILABLE` with `retry_after_ms`; the client retries with a fresh `getChannelCall`. *Amendment 2026-10-13 (task-3 review M4):* a start whose row lands just before the close can instead answer `201` and see the call end at once (the pass re-reads after the close and ends a call left on a closed group); the client retries the same way.
  - **Attacker question.** *Can a member force-close a live call?* No: the sweep never touches a group a live session names; ending a live call stays `DELETE /v1/calls/{id}` by a current leaf, as today. *Can an attacker keep a poisoned group open to refuse honest joiners?* Only by keeping a call live on it: a call nobody joins is reaped after 300 s, which ends it and closes the group (`endCall`), and a forged leaf that is not in the room is removed by the room sweep after its 30 s join window (`callmembers.go`). A forged device that does connect keeps the refusal up while it stays; the recovery is moderation (a kick or ban removes its leaves), recorded as residual risk. *Can an attacker get an honest, idle-but-about-to-be-used group closed?* Only by time: it costs the honest client one re-registration and one retry.
- **L-GW-32** No change to ops 50 and 52. The client rule: read the snapshot (L-HTTP-100 or 101), then apply the `voice_state` frames received during the read, in order. A frame adds an unknown device; `flags` 0 removes it.
- **L-GW-33** S→C op **53 `call_ring`** `[call_id(bstr16), channel_id(bstr16), user_id(bstr16), state(uint 0..2), expires_ts(uint)]`, with `n=0` and `group_id=null`. It is never persisted or replayed, and a client never sends it (`E_FRAME_TYPE`).
  - **States:**
    - `1` ringing: `user_id` is the caller.
    - `0` ring over: answered by one of the recipient's devices, declined by the recipient, the call ended, or it timed out. `user_id` is the caller.
    - `2` declined: sent to the caller's devices, and `user_id` is the participant who declined.
  - **`expires_ts`:** unix seconds when the ring ends. It is `now + 30` at ring start, and repeats that same value in state 0 and state 2.
  - **Server rules:**
    - A start that **opens** a call (`201`) on a channel of kind 3 or 4 rings every other participant user who has no device in the call. Each of that user's online devices gets state 1.
    - A user's ring stops (state 0 to all that user's devices) at the first of these: a device of that user is admitted to the call's room (the webhook `joined`); the call ends; 30 s pass (the instance clock); or the user declines.
    - There is at most one ring per (call, user). A `200` start of a live call never rings.
    - **Rate rule (anti-harassment, review fix).** Any user may open a DM with any account on the instance (`09` § DMs) and any group-DM participant may add anyone, so without a bound a caller could ring someone without end by starting, ending and restarting calls, or by opening many group DMs. The instance rings a given (caller user, recipient user) pair at most once per 15 s and at most 20 times per rolling hour, across all channels. A start beyond the bound still opens the call (`201`) but sends that recipient no op 53, and logs one `INFO` per suppressed pair per hour. The bound is keyed by the pair only, never by the recipient alone, so no third party can spend a recipient's budget and silence honest callers (lesson e). The state lives in the `Ringer`'s memory, capped at 65,536 pairs with oldest-first eviction.
      - *Amendment 2026-10-13 (task-3 review M1, controller ruling):* a pair whose window has passed is dropped first; one caller user holds at most 1,024 live pairs, and a new recipient beyond that share is not rung (fail closed for that caller only); only a table full of live pairs evicts the oldest live pair, which takes at least 64 callers at their full share (residual). The pair rule measures time on the monotonic clock (review M5).
      - *Amendment 2026-10-13 (task-3 review I2, controller ruling):* one caller user has at most 8 calls ringing at once (RV-4's "8 concurrent rings per client", enforced server side); a 9th start by that caller rings nobody, for that caller only. Every path that ends a call stops its rings (review I1), and only the start whose write opened the call's room rings (review M2).
    - A caller whose device leaves an otherwise empty room ends the call, which stops every ring.
    - A client's decoder refuses a malformed op 53, or one with state > 2, as `E_FRAME_SHAPE` and drops it without disconnecting. Sent by a client, any op 53 is `E_FRAME_TYPE` at the instance (an S→C opcode). The two vector rejects carry the instance's code, `E_FRAME_TYPE`, as `server opcode from a client` does, and client-core's `CLIENT_VERDICT` pins `E_FRAME_SHAPE` for both.

## New refusals and their attackers (lesson e)

"Can an attacker trigger this refusal against an honest party?" for every refusal web-3 adds:

| refusal | who can trigger it | against an honest party? | accepted because |
|---|---|---|---|
| Join refused, or mid-call leave, on a failed leaf (`call_gate` 1–2: unlisted, bad credential, tier) | the instance; **any member** who joins the call group with a device their own list does not name | **Yes**: one malicious member stops voice in that channel for the others while the leaf stays. | It is the security property (no audio to an unvalidated leaf). Recovery: the call group closes when the call ends (`endCall`); a forged leaf not in the room is removed by the room sweep after 30 s; an idle poisoned group is closed after 10 min (L-HTTP-105, RV-2); a forged device that stays connected is removed by moderation (residual). |
| Mid-call pause on an identity change (`E_UMK_CHANGED`, RV-3) | the instance; any member whose identity changes, honestly or not | **Yes**: everyone who has not verified that member hears and sends nothing in that call until they verify or the member leaves. | The call does not end for anyone, nobody is forced out, and no key reaches the changed device either way. At join the change still refuses (`voice.blockedChanged`). |
| Idle call-group close (L-HTTP-105, RV-2) | only time | no live call can be closed by it (`TestTheIdleCloseNeverTouchesALiveCallsGroup`); a start racing it costs one retry | — |
| `E_DEVICE_LIST_STALE` at join or key derivation (RV-1) | the instance, by withholding a signed list | yes, as a denial of service | the instance can deny service anyway; a member cannot withhold another member's list |
| DM call refused while the DM text group is blocked (decision 4) | the instance; the other DM participant by rotating keys | yes, until the person compares safety numbers | binding founder decision; same behaviour as the DM's paused composer |
| Ring dropped for a blocked DM | the instance | yes (a missed ring) | the instance can withhold op 53 anyway |
| Ring dropped when the caller is not a participant / unknown channel / 9th ring | the instance | no honest ring is dropped: the server only rings participants, and eight concurrent rings is far above any honest load | — |
| Pair ring rate rule (15 s / 20 per hour) | only the caller themself | no: keyed by (caller, recipient), so nobody can spend another caller's budget | a recipient-only cap was rejected for exactly that reason |
| Decline with a stale `expires_ts` is a no-op | — | no | — |
| `404` on the read routes and decline for non-viewers / non-participants | — | no | answers like an unknown id |
| `E_CALL_ACTIVE` | local only | no | — |
| `CallTile` hides media of an unverified stream | the SFU or instance injecting a participant | no | — |

## Spec and code conflicts found while planning

1. **Spec L514, "Voice requires `RTCRtpScriptTransform`".** The code keeps Chromium on `createEncodedStreams` (DEV-28 (a)). This plan follows the code.
2. **Spec L512 puts the voice controller in client-core,** but WebRTC cannot run in a worker. The `Room` therefore lives on the page, and B1's "only the core holds keys" is weakened for `base_key` (decision 3, the 08 edit).
3. **The browser core has no call groups.** `ClientCore` hard-codes `GroupKind::Text` and `media_version 0` (`core/dilla-core/src/client/groups.rs:95-110`), and the client paths assume one group per `target_id`. Task 5 fixes this.
4. **protocol/09 has no way to find a voice channel's call group, and no snapshot of who is in voice.** `text_group_id` is always null for voice (`09:326-331`), and `voice_state` is push-only. Task 3 adds both.
5. **The brief's "M mute, D deafen" contradicts web-2c's no-single-key rule.** Decision 1 resolved it, and task 1 adds a dated note under the brief's keyboard line.
6. **The brief said sounds are off by default in the browser tier.** Round 2 R4 makes them on, and task 1 amends the brief with a dated note.
7. **`Permissions-Policy: camera=(), microphone=(), display-capture=()` and `media-src 'none'`** (`internal/web/web.go:26-28`, `09:1275-1276`) block `getUserMedia` and attachment players outright. Task 3 widens both.
8. **The wasm budget was nearly exhausted**, and `@dilla/media` loads a second full copy of the core, although the spec described a small media-only wasm (MD-4). Decision 5 and task 8 address it.
9. **The testhost's LiveKit URL is `wss://<domain>`** (`internal/dillad/routes.go:132`), which a 127.0.0.1 browser test cannot reach. Task 3 adds a test-host override.
10. **Some client obligations exist in no client:**
    - the self-`Remove` before disconnect (DEV-43, NV-9);
    - the 60-minute call-leaf rotation (protocol/01:194);
    - WebAudio per-user playback (spec L502, card 12);
    - the voice verification code UI (spec L749).

    Tasks 5, 6, 8 and 9 implement them.
11. **The spec has no DM ringing at all.** Decision 6 adds it, and needs a new S→C op 53 and a decline route (task 1, task 3).
12. **The deploy guide's Pangolin UDP section says "the 30-second value is pending the founder-path measurement"** (`docs/deploy/README.md:136`, media card 6). Task 2 measures it.
13. **LiveKit's 3 s `subscriberUpdateInterval`** delays joiner visibility by about 2.9 s (media card 23), and the spec is silent about it.
14. **The brief uses "call" only for DM calls, while the protocol and API say "call" everywhere.** The copy follows the brief.
